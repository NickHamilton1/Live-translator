# Design Guardrails: Spider-Man-Adjacent T-Shirt Line

**Purpose:** A one-page creative brief for the design team. Follow these rules to stay on the safe side of trademark and copyright law. This is NOT legal advice — consult a trademark attorney before finalizing any designs.

**Core Principle:** We are making *spider-themed, movie-night, superhero-nostalgia merch* — NOT Spider-Man merch. Every design must stand on its own without relying on Marvel's IP.

---

## HARD NOs — Never Use These

| Category | Specific Examples | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Character names** | Spider-Man, Spiderman, Spidey, Peter Parker, Miles Morales, Gwen Stacy, MJ, Venom | All trademarked by Marvel Characters, Inc. |
| **Movie titles** | No Way Home, Homecoming, Far From Home, Into the Spider-Verse, Brand New Day | Trademarked; directly identifies the films |
| **Trademarked phrases** | "With great power comes great responsibility" | Registered in Class 25 (shirts) specifically — Reg. 5,054,732 |
| **Brand phrases** | "Friendly Neighborhood Spider-Man," "Amazing Spider-Man," "Ultimate Spider-Man" | All registered trademarks |
| **Marvel's spider logos** | The stylized chest spider, back spider, or any close imitation | Trademarked in Class 25 (apparel) via 2016 filings |
| **Character likenesses** | The mask, costume, web-shooters, specific poses, eye shapes | Copyright-protected character design |
| **Fan art** | Even "original" drawings of Spider-Man in any style (chibi, minimalist, abstract) | Derivative works — infringes copyright |
| **Other Marvel IP** | Avengers, MCU, Marvel, any other character names or logos | Same enforcement regime |

---

## SAFE TO USE — Green Light

| Element | Guidelines | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Generic spider imagery** | Realistic spiders, anatomical illustrations, tarantulas — must NOT resemble Marvel's stylized logos | A realistic black widow spider, a geometric spider design, a cute cartoon spider that looks nothing like Marvel's |
| **Spider-web patterns** | Geometric webs, cobwebs, silk motifs — these are generic and uncopyrightable | Web-patterned background, corner cobweb accent, abstract web geometry |
| **Red & blue color schemes** | Color combinations alone are not protectable | Red/blue tie-dye, color-block designs, gradient backgrounds |
| **Generic superhero language** | Broad terms about heroism, bravery, movie nights | "Hero," "Superhero," "Saving the day," "Cape not included" |
| **Original spider characters** | Your own spider mascot/character that looks nothing like Spider-Man | A spider wearing sunglasses, a spider with a top hat, a spider sitting in a movie theater |
| **Movie-night / theater themes** | Popcorn, movie tickets, theater seats, "opening night" vibes | "Opening Night Crew," "Midnight Showing," ticket stub graphics |
| **Year/era references (careful)** | Referencing years without naming movies | "Since 2002," "Est. 2002" — but see Yellow zone below |
| **Couple/family themes** | "His & Hers," "Date Night," "Family Movie Night" | Matching set text, heart imagery, family silhouettes |

---

## YELLOW ZONE — Use With Caution

These are gray areas. They're probably defensible but could still trigger automated takedowns or aggressive C&D letters from Disney's legal team. Use sparingly and understand the risk.

| Element | Risk Level | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Vague era references** ("Class of 2002," "The 2002 Era") | Medium | Doesn't name the movie, but context + marketing could imply it. Avoid pairing with spider imagery. |
| **"Bitten by destiny" / "Bitten by fate"** | Low-Medium | Original phrase, but clearly evocative. No trademark on this. Safer than most yellow items. |
| **"Web-slinger" without "Spider-Man"** | Medium-High | Not trademarked on its own, but strongly associated. Disney could argue common-law trademark. |
| **"Friendly neighborhood [anything]"** | High | "Your Friendly Neighborhood Spider-Man" is filed as a trademark. Even the fragment "friendly neighborhood" is strongly associated. Avoid. |
| **Generic spider silhouettes** | Low-Medium | Safe if clearly different from Marvel's specific designs. Risk increases if it's on a red/blue background in a chest-placement position. |
| **"Great power / great responsibility" variations** | High | The exact phrase is trademarked. Even variations ("big power, big responsibility") could be seen as an attempt to evade. Avoid. |
| **Nostalgic quote-style designs** ("You know who I am") | Medium | Generic enough alone, but combined with spider themes, a reasonable consumer might think it's official merch. |

## VISUAL STYLE GUIDELINES

### DO:
- **Flat graphic design** with bold typography — let the text and layout carry the design
- **Retro/vintage aesthetic** — distressed textures, retro fonts, vintage poster style
- **Minimalist icons** — simple, original spider icons, web line art, geometric patterns
- **Pop art style** — color-blocked, Warhol-esque treatments of original imagery
- **Movie-night lifestyle imagery** — popcorn, tickets, theater marquees, reclining seats
- **Couple/family matching themes** — coordinated designs that work as sets

### DON'T:
- Don't place a spider on the chest of a red shirt (mimics the costume)
- Don't use the specific eye-shape from Spider-Man's mask (large, angular white eyes)
- Don't recreate any recognizable pose from the films or comics
- Don't use web-shooting hand gestures (the specific two-finger-and-thumb pose)
- Don't mimic the typography or logo style from any Spider-Man film
- Don't create designs where the overall impression reads as "Spider-Man costume"

## PRE-LAUNCH DESIGN CHECKLIST

Before any design goes to print, run it through this checklist:

- [ ] Does it contain ANY trademarked word, name, or phrase? → REJECT
- [ ] Does any visual element resemble Marvel's specific spider logos? → REJECT
- [ ] Could a reasonable consumer think this is officially licensed merch? → REDESIGN
- [ ] Does it include any character likeness, even stylized? → REJECT
- [ ] Is it paired with marketing copy that names Spider-Man or any film? → FIX MARKETING
- [ ] Would it survive if sold at a non-Spider-Man event? (i.e., is it a good spider/hero design on its own?) → If no, it's relying too much on the IP → REDESIGN

---

## MARKETING & LISTING RULES

The design might be clean, but the marketing can still create liability:

- **NEVER use "Spider-Man" in product titles, descriptions, tags, or SEO**
- **NEVER use movie titles in ad copy or product listings**
- **DO use:** "spider," "hero," "superhero," "movie night," "opening night," "summer blockbuster"
- **Ad targeting:** You can target Spider-Man fans via Meta's interest targeting (that's Meta's data, not your trademark use), but ad creative and copy must stay clean
- **If asked "is this Spider-Man merch?"** — the honest answer must be "no, it's original spider-themed movie-night merch." If that answer feels dishonest, the design is too close.

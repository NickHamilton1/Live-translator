# Risk Scenario Planning: Spider-Man-Adjacent T-Shirt Business

**Context:** Unofficial fan t-shirts timed to Spider-Man: Brand New Day (July 31, 2026). 100K+ units via Shopify, Meta ads, Virginia printer. Delaware LLC structure.

**Disclaimer:** This is not legal advice. Consult a trademark/IP attorney before proceeding.

---

## Scenario 1: Cease & Desist Received Mid-Campaign (Most Likely)

### Probability: HIGH if designs enter yellow/red zone; LOW-MEDIUM if strictly green

### How It Happens
- Disney's brand protection team (or automated monitoring service like MarkMonitor, Corsearch, or OpSec Security) flags your Shopify store or Meta ads
- A C&D letter arrives via email and/or certified mail to your LLC's registered agent
- Typical trigger: a design that's too close to Marvel IP, or marketing copy/SEO that uses trademarked terms

### Typical Timeline
| Event | Timeframe |
|---|---|
| Design goes live | Day 0 |
| Automated detection or manual report | Days 1–14 |
| C&D letter sent | Days 7–30 |
| Response deadline in C&D | 7–14 days from receipt |
| Follow-up / escalation if ignored | 14–30 days after deadline |
| Lawsuit filing if non-compliant | 30–90 days after C&D |

### What the C&D Typically Demands
1. **Immediately stop selling** all infringing products
2. **Remove all listings** from Shopify, social media, and ads
3. **Destroy remaining inventory** of infringing designs
4. **Provide an accounting** of all units sold and revenue generated
5. **Disgorge profits** from infringing sales
6. **Pay damages** (sometimes negotiated away if you comply quickly)
7. **Sign a consent agreement** promising never to infringe again

### Response Options
1. **Comply immediately** — Pull the specific designs, respond to the letter confirming compliance. Cheapest option. May lose some inventory but preserves the business for non-infringing designs.
2. **Negotiate** — Engage an attorney ($5K–$20K) to negotiate scope. Sometimes the C&D is overly broad and you can push back on designs that are genuinely non-infringing.
3. **Ignore** — Worst option. Leads to lawsuit. Never ignore a Disney C&D.

### Financial Impact
- Lost revenue from pulled designs: Variable (could be a single design or entire store)
- Attorney fees for response: $2,000–$10,000
- Inventory write-off on infringing designs: $3–7/unit × affected inventory
- Settlement payment if demanded: $5,000–$50,000 (typical small-seller range)
- **Total realistic cost: $10,000–$75,000**

## Scenario 2: Lawsuit Filed

### Probability: LOW if you comply with C&D; MEDIUM if you ignore or continue selling

### How It Happens
- You ignore the C&D, or continue selling after receiving it
- Your operation is large enough to attract attention ($600K+ in ad spend is very visible)
- Disney's outside counsel files in federal court (likely Central District of California or Southern District of New York)

### What You Face
- **Trademark infringement** under the Lanham Act (15 U.S.C. § 1114)
- **Copyright infringement** under the Copyright Act (17 U.S.C. § 501)
- **Unfair competition** and state law claims

### Potential Damages
| Damage Type | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Statutory damages (trademark)** | $1,000–$200,000 per mark | Up to $2,000,000 per mark for willful infringement |
| **Statutory damages (copyright)** | $750–$30,000 per work | Up to $150,000 per work for willful infringement |
| **Actual damages** | Plaintiff's lost profits + your profits | Disney can elect statutory OR actual damages |
| **Attorney's fees** | $50,000–$500,000+ | Court can award prevailing party's fees in trademark cases |
| **Injunction** | Immediate stop of all sales | Court order; violation = contempt |
| **Destruction of inventory** | Cost of remaining stock | Court-ordered destruction |

### Worst-Case Financial Exposure
- Multiple marks infringed (Spider-Man name + logo + catchphrase): 3 marks × $200K = **$600K statutory**
- Plus copyright claims on character likeness: **$150K**
- Plus attorney's fees: **$200K+**
- **Theoretical maximum: $1M+**
- **Realistic settlement for a small operator who lawyered up: $50K–$250K**

### Timeline
| Event | Timeframe |
|---|---|
| Complaint filed | Day 0 |
| Temporary restraining order (TRO) | Days 1–14 (can be ex parte) |
| Preliminary injunction hearing | 30–60 days |
| Discovery | 3–12 months |
| Trial or settlement | 12–24 months |

Most cases settle before trial. Disney prefers quick settlements with consent decrees over lengthy litigation.

## Scenario 3: Platform / Payment Processor Shutdown

### Probability: MEDIUM-HIGH if any IP complaint is filed

### Shopify
- Shopify complies with DMCA and trademark takedown notices
- A rights holder complaint can lead to: product removal → store suspension → permanent termination
- Shopify's AUP prohibits selling counterfeit goods; they can shut you down at their discretion
- **Timeline: store can be disabled within 24–72 hours of a complaint**
- Appeal process exists but is slow (weeks) and heavily favors the complainant

### Stripe / Payment Processors
- Stripe can freeze funds and hold them for up to **90–120 days** pending investigation
- If Stripe terminates you for IP infringement, you may be placed on the MATCH list (Member Alert to Control High-Risk Merchants), making it very difficult to get a new merchant account
- PayPal has similar freeze/hold policies with even less transparency
- **Frozen funds scenario: $50K–$200K+ in revenue held for months**

### Meta Ads
- Ad account can be disabled for policy violations (IP complaints)
- If your ad account is banned, you lose the ability to run ads — killing your entire acquisition channel
- Meta Business account bans can be permanent and very difficult to appeal

### Cascading Failure Risk
The nightmare scenario: Disney files complaints simultaneously with Shopify, Stripe, and Meta. Within 72 hours:
1. Shopify store goes dark — no new orders
2. Stripe freezes all funds — no access to revenue
3. Meta disables ad account — no way to drive traffic even if store comes back
4. You have inventory committed, printing costs paid, and zero way to sell or collect money

## Scenario 4: No Legal Action (Best Case)

### Probability: MODERATE if designs stay strictly in the green zone

### What Makes This More Likely
- Designs use only generic spider/web imagery with no Marvel-specific elements
- No trademarked words, names, or phrases anywhere — product, marketing, SEO, ads
- Operation is small enough or short-lived enough to fly under the radar
- No customer complaints or competitor reports to trigger investigation
- Designs don't go viral in a way that attracts Disney's attention

### What Makes This Less Likely
- $600K in Meta ad spend is VERY visible — Meta shares advertiser data with brand protection teams
- 100K+ units shipped creates a large footprint
- Social media virality (which you want for sales) also attracts IP enforcement
- Disney uses automated brand monitoring tools that scan Shopify stores, social media, and ad platforms continuously
- The Spider-Man: Brand New Day release window is exactly when Disney's monitoring is at maximum intensity

### Realistic Assessment
Even with clean designs, operating at this scale during a major movie release window carries inherent visibility risk. The question isn't whether Disney could find you — it's whether your designs are clean enough that finding you doesn't matter.

## LLC Structure & Liability Protection

### Delaware LLC Formation
- **Purpose:** Limits personal liability to the assets of the LLC
- **Formation time:** 24 hours with expedited filing ($100 extra) through the Delaware Division of Corporations
- **Cost:** $90 state fee + $100–$300/year registered agent + $300/year Delaware franchise tax
- **Total first-year cost: ~$500–$700**

### What the LLC Protects
- Personal assets (home, car, savings) are generally shielded from business debts and judgments
- Creditors and plaintiffs can only reach LLC assets
- You can't be personally named in a trademark lawsuit (in theory)

### What the LLC Does NOT Protect
- **Piercing the corporate veil:** Courts can hold you personally liable if:
  - You commingle personal and business funds
  - The LLC is undercapitalized (no real money in it)
  - You don't observe corporate formalities (no operating agreement, no separate bank account)
  - The LLC is used as a "mere instrumentality" to commit fraud or wrongdoing
- **Willful infringement:** If you knowingly infringe trademarks, a court is more likely to pierce the veil, especially for a single-member LLC that was clearly set up to limit exposure from an intentionally infringing business
- **Personal guarantees:** If you personally guarantee Stripe, Shopify, or any vendor accounts, the LLC doesn't help for those obligations

### Practical Reality
An LLC provides meaningful protection against accidental liability but limited protection against intentional trademark infringement. If Disney can show you knew you were trading on their IP (and this entire research document would be evidence of that knowledge), the LLC may not shield you.

## Licensing Alternative Analysis

### Can You Get a Marvel/Disney License?

**Short answer: Almost certainly not for this use case.**

### Requirements (Disney Consumer Products)
- Typically requires 5+ years of business history
- Must demonstrate ability to generate six-figure annual royalties (implying $1M+ in product sales)
- Royalty rates: 15–30% of wholesale price
- Non-exclusive license for specific product category, territory, and term
- Legal negotiation costs: $100K–$150K+
- Application review timeline: 3–6 months minimum

### Why It Won't Work Here
1. **Timeline:** 3–6 months to get a license; you need to be selling by mid-June
2. **Business history:** New LLC with no track record won't qualify
3. **Economics:** At 20% royalty on $22 retail, that's ~$4.40/unit — destroys the margin model
4. **Scale commitment:** Disney expects ongoing business, not a 6-week pop-up

### Alternative: Licensed Wholesaler
- Buy officially licensed Spider-Man shirts from an authorized wholesaler (Mad Engine, Bioworld, Hybrid Apparel)
- Resell at markup
- Legal risk: Zero (products are already licensed)
- Downside: No custom designs, lower margins, competing with everyone else selling the same shirts
- Could work as a supplement to original designs, not as the core strategy

## Decision Framework: Risk/Reward Assessment

### The Core Tension
The business model's profit depends on being associated with Spider-Man without paying for a license. The legal risk is directly proportional to how strongly the merch evokes Spider-Man.

### Risk Matrix

| Design Approach | Revenue Potential | Legal Risk | Net Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Green zone only** (generic spiders, no IP references) | Lower — harder to market without the association | Very Low | Safest but may not hit 100K unit target |
| **Green + Yellow zone** (evocative but no direct IP use) | Medium — rides the wave without naming it | Low-Medium | Best risk/reward balance |
| **Yellow + Red zone** (names, likenesses, quotes) | Highest — direct fan appeal | Very High | Almost certain C&D; potential lawsuit |
| **Licensed wholesale resale** | Low margins, limited differentiation | Zero | Safe but doesn't justify the infrastructure |

### Go / No-Go Criteria

**GREEN LIGHT — Proceed if:**
- All designs pass the checklist in `03-design-guardrails.md`
- No trademarked terms in any customer-facing content
- Attorney reviews top designs before production
- LLC properly formed with separate bank account
- Contingency plan for pulling designs within 24 hours

**YELLOW LIGHT — Proceed with caution if:**
- Some designs are in the yellow zone
- You accept the risk of losing those specific designs mid-campaign
- You have backup green-zone designs ready to swap in
- Budget includes $20K legal contingency fund

**RED LIGHT — Do not proceed if:**
- Core designs rely on trademarked names, character likenesses, or movie titles
- The marketing strategy requires using "Spider-Man" in ad copy or SEO
- You can't afford $50K+ in potential legal costs
- You can't absorb a complete inventory write-off if the store gets shut down

### Break-Even on Legal Risk
At $7.50 profit per unit:
- A $50K legal settlement wipes out the profit from ~6,700 units
- A $250K worst-case wipes out ~33,300 units
- At 100K units ($750K profit), even a $250K settlement leaves $500K — still profitable but painful
- At 10K units ($75K profit), a $50K settlement leaves almost nothing

**The math only works if you sell enough volume to absorb potential legal costs, AND your designs are clean enough to make legal action unlikely.**

## Recommended Approach

1. **Form the Delaware LLC** — minimal cost, meaningful protection, do it regardless
2. **Stay in the green zone** for all designs — use the guardrails strictly
3. **Have a trademark attorney review** your top 25 designs before production ($2K–$5K)
4. **Build a "kill switch"** — ability to pull any design from Shopify in minutes
5. **Don't put all eggs in one basket** — diversify designs so losing one doesn't kill revenue
6. **Budget $20K for legal contingency** — C&D response, attorney consultation
7. **Consider supplementing with licensed wholesale** — mix in a few officially licensed shirts for zero-risk revenue
8. **Monitor for takedowns actively** — check Shopify, Meta, and email daily during the campaign
9. **Keep clean records** — separate bank account, operating agreement, all expenses documented
